## PART 2 — NEW SPECIMEN: the class hierarchy (the four damages)

Mishnah Bava Kamma 1:1 opens the damages order with a MODULE MAP:
"There are FOUR primary categories of damage: Ox, Pit, Maveh
(grazing/tooth — the gemara records the two readings), and Fire."
Now set that beside the keyword scan of Exodus 21-22 from
REPORT_CODE_HUNT.md: the case openers after the injury laws are —
the goring ox (KI, 21:28 and 21:35), the pit (KI, 21:33), the
grazing beast (KI, 22:4), the fire (KI, 22:5). **The Mishnah's four
primary categories are the code's four consecutive KI cases.** The
answer key's module list is the case list, in order.

And the Mishnah's own next words are INTERFACE ANALYSIS (pulled at
Bava Kamma 2a:2-3): "the defining characteristic of Ox is not
similar to Maveh... and these, whose typical manner is to proceed
and damage, are not similar to Pit, which does not proceed" —
pairwise comparison of the classes' properties, closing with the
shared interface: their common feature is that their way is to
damage, their safeguarding is on you, and the liable party pays
from the best of the land — one common output rule over four
classes. The gemara's very first move (2a) is subclass typing: the
word "primary categories" (avot, literally "fathers") implies
DERIVATIVES (toladot, "offspring") — and the opening pages sort
which derivatives inherit their parent's rules. A class hierarchy
with inheritance, argued in the tradition's own vocabulary.

## PART 3 — NEW SPECIMEN: the timer and the cross-module interrupt

The Hebrew slave (code: Exodus 21:2, KI-case with the six-year
timer in the ink: "six years he shall serve, and in the seventh he
goes free"). The answer key (Mishnah Kiddushin 1:2): "A Hebrew
slave is acquired through money or document, and acquires himself
through YEARS, through JUBILEE, or through DEDUCTION of money."
Look at that release list: the first entry is the code's own timer;
the third is a computed buy-out (pay the remaining years); and the
middle one — the Jubilee — is not in Exodus at all. It is an event
declared in a DIFFERENT module (Leviticus 25), a global interrupt
that frees slaves everywhere when it fires. The teacher documents
the wiring (pulled at Kiddushin 15a:19): the Torah "needed to
write" the Jubilee release even for the pierced slave — the one who
chose to stay "forever" — because neither case follows from the
other. A cross-module event overriding a local "forever": the
answer key's release list is a merge of two modules' code, and the
Talmud shows the merge.

## PART 4 — NEW SPECIMEN: the diagnostic loop (a Leviticus preview)

Leviticus 13 (the skin-mark examination — the book we read next) is
a bounded LOOP, and the machine scan sees it in the ink. Scanning
13:1-8 for the loop keywords:

- 13:3 — condemn branch: the deep-mark test fires → IMPURE.
- 13:4 — else: QUARANTINE + SEVEN days (iteration one).
- 13:5 — re-examine; QUARANTINE + SEVEN days + the word
  שֵׁנִית ("a SECOND time") — **the loop's iteration counter,
  written in the ink.**
- 13:6 — re-examine "a second time"; faded → PURE (loop exits
  clean, bounded at two passes).
- 13:7-8 — but if it spreads after the exit → IMPURE (re-entry
  clause).

A diagnostic algorithm with branch conditions, a timed wait, an
explicit iteration counter, a bounded loop, and a re-entry guard —
and the Mishnah's tractate Negaim compiles its case tables (the
shades, the marks, the quarantine outcomes) exactly the way the
answer key compiles the guardians. When we open Sifra on Leviticus,
this function is waiting in chapter 13, and the first recorded CALL
into the Exodus machine is waiting in chapter 24.
