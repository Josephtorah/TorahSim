  The children of Israel are commanded to give the Levites forty-eight cities with pasture measured two thousand cubits on each side, six of them cities of refuge; the man who strikes with iron, stone or wood, or in enmity, is a murderer and dies by the avenger's hand after the court; the man who strikes suddenly without enmity flees to the city and dwells there until the death of the high priest, then returns to his land; two witnesses, no ransom for a life or for an exile, and the land is not to be polluted with blood, for the LORD dwells in its midst. The Sifrei returns at 35:9 for its last three sections, the Mishnah's Makkot is the answer sheet whole, and the Talmud reaches the Sabbath's two thousand cubits from this chapter's measure by a chain of eight analogies. "In whose midst I dwell" stands at 5:3 and here alone in the Torah, the book's inclusio computed.
