## The story in one paragraph

Bereshit Rabbah is not a scrapbook of sayings. It is a **long commentary that walks Genesis in order**, with a **heavy front** that installs how to think (plan, tiers, multi-views, parables, operators), a **large middle** of patriarch application, and a **late Joseph / tribal bulk** where page weight and proof density both peak. Almost every unit **proves from Scripture**; a shared set of midrash “glue words” runs the whole book; a few verses and a few rabbis get most of the traffic. Early chapters **import widely** from Psalms, Isaiah, Job, Proverbs (and other Torah) to land on Genesis; later chapters cite Genesis itself more, while still calling those same outside books as a shared library. God is staged with a **title stack** (creation often *Elohim*; running midrash prefers “the Holy One blessed be He”; presence language rises later). Persons light up **when the Genesis spine reaches them**, plus a smaller import cast (Moses, David) for proof and typology. True stacked “another interpretation” is rare; a **chorus of rabbis** is common. The book ends in mourning / family close energy, not a secret-code finale.

## How to read the next BR unit (zoom-in checklist)

When opening the next sequential packet (next up ~**BR 3:2**):

1. **Where on the spine?** Still creation / light zone, or already shifting?  
2. **Boot or app?** Is this installing a method, or applying one?  
3. **Operators present?** Proof glue, *zeh/zu*, *davar aher*, mashal, question words?  
4. **Who speaks?** Rabbi chorus, HKBH, angel, other?  
5. **What is imported?** Only Genesis, or Psalms/Isaiah/Job/… landing here?  
6. **Hub or leaf?** One-off verse, or a line we already know is reused book-wide?  
7. **Persons / names?** Treat as open-minded hubs — not only historical labels.  
8. **Don’t force the whole OS** into every line — use the map; let the unit surprise you.

Sequential packets remain the **stress test**. If a unit breaks this story, update the map — don’t silence the unit.
