## 1. What we built (in one breath)

We followed **one thin storyline** through the Torah:

1. **Exodus** builds a special tent and turns it “on.”  
2. **Leviticus 1** runs a sacrifice procedure **using** that tent — without rebuilding it.  
3. **Numbers** uses the **cloud** on that tent to decide when Israel **camps** or **marches**.  
4. **Deuteronomy** talks about **“the place God will choose”** for worship in the land — a new way of talking about the *idea* of one authorized holy place.

The demo asks: *Does later Torah keep using names that earlier Torah set up?*  
For this slice, the answer is **yes**.

---

## 2. A kitchen picture (no jargon)

Imagine a **kitchen**:

| Stage | Kitchen | Torah (this demo) |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| Build | Install oven, counter, sink | Exodus: tent, altar, priests, cloud |
| Cook | Recipe says “use the oven” | Leviticus: bring offering to the tent entrance, priests use the altar |
| Move house | GPS says when to leave / stop | Numbers: cloud lifts → go; cloud stays → camp |
| New house rules | “Cook only in the kitchen we pick in the new city” | Deuteronomy: “the place the Lord will choose” |

You do **not** rebuild the oven in every recipe.  
You **name** it and use it.

## 4. The four chapters of the demo (story)

### Chapter A — Exodus builds and turns the system on

In plain story order:

1. God says: make Me a **sanctuary** so I can dwell among them  
   (מִקְדָּשׁ / *mikdash* / “sanctuary”).  
2. Build according to a **pattern**  
   (תַּבְנִית / *tavnit* / “blueprint”).  
3. Design the **altar**.  
4. The place is called the **Tent of Meeting**.  
5. **Aaron’s sons** are appointed as **priests**.  
6. They are brought to the **entrance of the Tent**.  
7. God says He will **meet and speak** there.  
8. The altar is placed at that entrance.  
9. Finally a **cloud** covers the Tent and God’s **glory** fills the dwelling — like flipping the power switch **on**.

After that, the tent is not a sketch on paper. It is **live**.

**Where to read the detail:** [WRITES](DEMO_sanctuary_spine_v1_WRITES_2026-07-25.md)

---

### Chapter B — Leviticus 1 runs a recipe *using* the tent

Leviticus does **not** say “first, build a tent.”  
It assumes the tent is already there.

**Simple cattle burnt-offering path (Lev 1:1–9):**

1. God speaks to Moses **from the Tent of Meeting**.  
2. If someone brings an offering: it can come from cattle or flock (we follow **cattle** only in this demo).  
3. If it is a **burnt offering** (עֹלָה / *olah*) from cattle: male, unblemished.  
4. Bring it to the **entrance of the Tent**.  
5. The person **leans a hand** on the animal’s head.  
6. The person **slaughters**; the **priests** put **blood** on the **altar** at that entrance.  
7. Skin and cut the animal.  
8. Priests put **fire and wood** on the altar.  
9. Priests arrange the pieces on the fire.  
10. Wash the insides; the priest turns everything to smoke; closing formula of acceptance.

**Who does what (easy map):**

| Person who offers | Priests |
|-------------------|---------|
| Bring, lean, slaughter, flay, cut, wash | Blood on altar, fire, arrange pieces, smoke |

The **cantillation tree** (musical marks that structure the verse) often puts “slaughter” on one side of the verse and “priests + blood + altar” on the other — so the split is in the **text structure**, not only in a modern outline.

**Where to read the detail:** [OLAH_CATTLE](DEMO_sanctuary_spine_v1_OLAH_CATTLE_2026-07-25.md)

### Chapter C — Numbers: the cloud says stay or go

Same cloud that covered the tent at the end of Exodus now **runs the schedule**:

- While the cloud **sits** on the dwelling → Israel **camps**.  
- When the cloud is **lifted** → they **journey**.  
- Where the cloud **settles** → they **camp there**.  
- Always framed as **by the Lord’s command**.

Example of a real trip start (Num 10):

- Cloud lifts from the dwelling at Sinai.  
- Israel marches.  
- Cloud settles in the wilderness of Paran.

**Kitchen picture:** the cloud is like a **“open / closed” sign** for travel, attached to the same holy dwelling.

**Where to read the detail:** [CLOUD_FSM](DEMO_sanctuary_spine_v1_CLOUD_FSM_2026-07-25.md)

### Chapter D — Deuteronomy: new words for the land

In the land, Deuteronomy stresses:

> Seek **the place the Lord your God will choose**  
> (הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר־יִבְחַר / *ha-makom asher-yivchar*).

Important honesty in this demo:

- That is **not** the exact same Hebrew phrase as “Tent of Meeting.”  
- We still treat it as related **role**: one authorized center of God’s name / worship.  
- We call that **recompile** — like rewriting the address for a new country, not pretending the string is identical.

**Where to read the detail:** [USES](DEMO_sanctuary_spine_v1_USES_2026-07-25.md) § on *makom yivchar*

## 5. “Variable” in plain English

In this project a **variable** (free name) means:

> A **Hebrew name or phrase** the Torah uses again later  
> **without rebuilding** what it points to.

Examples:

- “Entrance of the Tent” is **set up** in Exodus and **used** in Leviticus.  
- “Cloud” is **turned on** in Exodus and **used for travel** in Numbers.

### What is *not* a variable by itself

Small glue words like:

| Hebrew | How to say | Role |
|--------|------------|------|
| אֵת | *et* | Points at a definite object (“take **et** the blood”) |
| אֶל | *el* | “to” (to the entrance…) |
| עַל | *al* | “on” (on the altar…) |

They are more like **arrows** than like names of furniture.

### Seed vs install (simple)

| Word | Example | Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Seed** | Noah builds an altar long before the Tabernacle | Same *kind* of word earlier; not yet the national sanctuary system |
| **Install** | Exodus designs and places the Tabernacle altar | The system Leviticus actually uses |
| **Use** | Leviticus puts blood on “the altar” | Reads the installed name |

## 6. How we found these links (no magic)

We did **not** start from a theory and force verses to fit.

Rough order:

1. Pick a short list of verses (the charter).  
2. Read the **Hebrew**.  
3. Use the **tree** of each verse (how cantillation splits it).  
4. List important **names**.  
5. Ask: where was this name **set up** earlier? where is it **used** later?  
6. Write that down with confidence labels (sure / hypothesis / open).

Sometimes you notice a name when you **use** it first (Leviticus), then **look back** to Exodus.  
That is normal — like finding a function name in app code, then jumping to where it was defined.

3. What tells Israel to march in Numbers?
