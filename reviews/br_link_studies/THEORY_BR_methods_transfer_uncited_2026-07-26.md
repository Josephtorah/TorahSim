## 1. The idea (one paragraph)

**Bereshit Rabbah does not have to cite a Genesis verse for that verse to be expandable.**  
BR trains **methods** (how to open thin headers, hinge roots, package light, dual-rail, multi-cite land, polyroot family…).  
Those methods are **transferable**: apply them to Gen verses that are **never tagged and never bare-quoted** in BR, then **derive** further logic from **Written** structure + remote Tanakh ports.  
BR is the **curriculum**; the **source text** of the derived logic is still the Hebrew verse (and its trees / absolute ports) — not a silent merge of midrash into Written.

## 6. Compile Genesis? (full-stack answer — short)

**No — not from BR alone, and not yet from BR methods + our current extracts.**

| Layer | Have enough? | Notes |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| **Source / AST** | Written yes | Gen trees + 1533 STEP draft exist **from ta’amim / Pre-Code**, not from BR |
| **Linker (L1)** | Partial | Absolute ports, some packages; not a full Gen link graph |
| **Manual (L2 BR)** | Partial curriculum | Dense on cosmogony hubs; ~¼ Gen never tagged; methods catalog incomplete |
| **Stdlib / Nakh ports** | Partial | Light, fruitfulness, amon samples — not every opcode |
| **Type checker / scenarios** | Sparse | Few frozen dual-track scenario suites for whole Gen |
| **Single executable “Genesis.out”** | **No** | Multi-voice aggadah ≠ deterministic compiler output |

**Enough for:** boot-week **method training**, hub demos, Path A/B probes, progressive instrumentation.  
**Not enough for:** one-shot **compile Genesis 1–50** as a closed program whose every verse is resolved by BR citation or BR-only rules.

**One line:**  
*BR gives a strong partial compiler course for Genesis links; Genesis itself (Written + trees) remains the codebase. You can ship modules; you cannot yet claim a full BR-driven build of the book.*
