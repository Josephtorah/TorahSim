## Plain English summary (read this first)

This midrash says: **studying how God created the world is not a stage for showing off.**  
If you teach “the Work of Creation” to look clever, you insult God’s honor.  
Also: if you talk crudely as if the world were built on trash (on chaos/junk), you insult the “palace.”  
Some hard phrases appear only because the Bible itself wrote them — that is not a license for careless talk.

## 1. Retell (plain English)

**Who is speaking:** Rav Huna, **passing on a teaching from** Bar Kappara (opens and closes). Rabbi Yose bar Hanina is cited on honor.

**Opening verse (Psalms 31:19), free gloss:**  
“Let lying lips be silenced…”  
The midrash plays with words in that verse (mute / deaf / silent), then gets to its real point.

**Main charge about “Work of Creation” teaching:**

- **Pride:** It is shocking if someone teaches creation **in order to boast**: “**I** expound the Work of Creation.”  
- **Contempt:** It is shocking if that person **despises God’s honor**.  
- Support: Anyone who glorifies himself by shaming another person has no share in the world to come — **all the more** if the honor at stake is God’s.  
- Next verse (Psalms 31:20), free gloss: “How great is Your goodness stored up for those who fear You” — for people who **fear**, not people who **despise Your awe**. Such a boaster should not share that stored goodness.

**Palace parable (plain English):**

A human king builds a palace on sewers, trash, and filth. Anyone who goes around saying “this palace is built on sewers and trash” **insults** the palace.  
In the same way: anyone who goes around saying “this world was created **out of** formless-void junk (tohu and bohu)” — **is that not an insult?**

**Closing:**  
If Scripture had not written it, one could not say it. Genesis says God created… and Genesis also says the earth was tohu va-vohu.  
So: the **Bible forces** those hard words onto the page; **human boasting and crude speech** about them can still violate honor.
