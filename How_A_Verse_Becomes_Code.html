How a Verse Becomes Code

Lesson One · the Second Passover · the workshop, 2026-09-03 · a living teaching file — we will refine it together

The one law this whole lesson obeys (your ruling): the code comes from the written verses alone. The Mishnah is never inside the code. The Mishnah gives us two things instead: the shape of the inputs our code must accept, and the test data — cases with expected verdicts — that we feed in at run time to grade what we wrote.
Reading the citations on this page. “Numbers 9:10” = the book of Numbers, chapter 9, verse 10. “Mishnah Pesachim 9:2” = the Mishnah (the rabbinic law code, compiled around the year 200, organized in 63 topical tractates), the tractate Pesachim (“Passovers”), chapter 9, paragraph 2. Every Hebrew quotation on this page carries its English directly beneath it, and every Mishnah paragraph we use appears IN FULL — no summaries.

STEP 1Read the verses — this is the code source

The whole law lives in a handful of verses of Numbers 9. Read them as a programmer reads a specification. The story part (verses 6–8) is itself remarkable: the law is born from a case — men show up whom the published rule doesn't cover, Moses holds the case and queries, and the new code arrives. The written text records its own patch protocol.

Numbers 9:2–3 — the base rule
וְיַעֲשׂוּ בְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת הַפָּסַח בְּמוֹעֲדוֹ׃ בְּאַרְבָּעָה עָשָׂר יוֹם בַּחֹדֶשׁ הַזֶּה בֵּין הָעַרְבַּיִם תַּעֲשׂוּ אֹתוֹ בְּמֹעֲדוֹ כְּכָל חֻקֹּתָיו וּכְכָל מִשְׁפָּטָיו תַּעֲשׂוּ אֹתוֹ
“And the children of Israel shall perform the Passover at its appointed time. On the FOURTEENTH DAY of THIS MONTH, at TWILIGHT, you shall perform it at its appointed time; according to ALL ITS STATUTES and all its ordinances you shall perform it.”
What the machine takes: the base schedule — month 1, day 14, twilight — and a pointer to the statute set (“all its statutes”). This is the default branch.
Numbers 9:6 — a case arrives that the code doesn't yet handle
וַיְהִי אֲנָשִׁים אֲשֶׁר הָיוּ טְמֵאִים לְנֶפֶשׁ אָדָם וְלֹא יָכְלוּ לַעֲשֹׂת הַפֶּסַח בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא וַיִּקְרְבוּ לִפְנֵי מֹשֶׁה וְלִפְנֵי אַהֲרֹן בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא
“And there were men who were IMPURE BY A HUMAN CORPSE, and they could not perform the Passover on that day; and they came before Moses and before Aaron on that day.”
A live input the published rule doesn't cover. The affected men file the case themselves.
Numbers 9:7 — the complaint, on the record
אֲנַחְנוּ טְמֵאִים לְנֶפֶשׁ אָדָם לָמָּה נִגָּרַע לְבִלְתִּי הַקְרִיב אֶת קָרְבַּן יְהוָה בְּמֹעֲדוֹ בְּתוֹךְ בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל
“We are impure by a human corpse — WHY SHOULD WE BE DIMINISHED, not to bring the LORD's offering at its appointed time among the children of Israel?”
The bug report, verbatim.
Numbers 9:8 — the query
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵהֶם מֹשֶׁה עִמְדוּ וְאֶשְׁמְעָה מַה יְצַוֶּה יְהוָה לָכֶם
“And Moses said to them: STAND, AND I WILL HEAR what the LORD commands concerning you.”
The runtime code-request. (The written Torah records this same hold-and-ask protocol four times: here, the blasphemer at Leviticus 24:12, the wood-gatherer at Numbers 15:34, the daughters of Zelophehad at Numbers 27:5.)
Numbers 9:10 — the new code arrives: the case opener
דַּבֵּר אֶל בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל לֵאמֹר אִישׁ אִישׁ כִּי יִהְיֶה טָמֵא לָנֶפֶשׁ אוֹ בְדֶרֶךְ רְחֹקָה לָכֶם אוֹ לְדֹרֹתֵיכֶם וְעָשָׂה פֶסַח לַיהוָה
“Speak to the children of Israel, saying: ANY MAN, WHEN [כִּי, ki] he is IMPURE BY A CORPSE, OR [אוֹ, o] on a DISTANT JOURNEY — for you or for your generations — he shall perform a Passover to the LORD.”
The case syntax we've measured across the law code: כִּי (“when”) opens the case; אוֹ (“or”) joins the second condition. Two inputs, one branch. And note: “distant” has NO NUMBER — hold that thought for Step 3.
Numbers 9:11–12 — the branch's output
בַּחֹדֶשׁ הַשֵּׁנִי בְּאַרְבָּעָה עָשָׂר יוֹם בֵּין הָעַרְבַּיִם יַעֲשׂוּ אֹתוֹ עַל מַצּוֹת וּמְרֹרִים יֹאכְלֻהוּ׃ לֹא יַשְׁאִירוּ מִמֶּנּוּ עַד בֹּקֶר וְעֶצֶם לֹא יִשְׁבְּרוּ בוֹ כְּכָל חֻקַּת הַפֶּסַח יַעֲשׂוּ אֹתוֹ
“In the SECOND month, on the fourteenth day, at twilight, they shall perform it; with unleavened bread and bitter herbs they shall eat it. They shall leave none of it until morning, nor break a bone of it; according to EVERY STATUTE of the Passover they shall perform it.”
The deferral output: new date (month 2, day 14), same time-of-day, same statute set — the code says “run the same statutes” rather than restating them: a subroutine call.
Numbers 9:13 — the else-branch
וְהָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר הוּא טָהוֹר וּבְדֶרֶךְ לֹא הָיָה וְחָדַל לַעֲשׂוֹת הַפֶּסַח וְנִכְרְתָה הַנֶּפֶשׁ הַהִוא מֵעַמֶּיהָ
“But the man who is PURE, and was NOT on a journey, and REFRAINS [וְחָדַל, ve-chadal] from performing the Passover — that soul shall be CUT OFF from its people...”
The negative branch, closed explicitly. Watch the third condition: חָדַל means a chosen desisting — a refusal. The penalty clause requires pure AND present AND refusing. That precision will matter when we meet the man who merely forgot.
Numbers 9:14 — the equal-input clause
וְכִי יָגוּר אִתְּכֶם גֵּר וְעָשָׂה פֶסַח לַיהוָה כְּחֻקַּת הַפֶּסַח וּכְמִשְׁפָּטוֹ כֵּן יַעֲשֶׂה חֻקָּה אַחַת יִהְיֶה לָכֶם וְלַגֵּר וּלְאֶזְרַח הָאָרֶץ
“And when a stranger sojourns with you and would perform a Passover to the LORD — according to the statute of the Passover and its ordinance, so shall he do; ONE STATUTE shall there be for you, and for the stranger, and for the native of the land.”
No separate branch for the stranger — the same function runs for him. In code, this is implemented by NOT writing a branch.

STEP 2See the structure the verses already have

Lay the verses out and the branching is visible — the same two-keyword case syntax (כִּי “when” opens, אוֹ “or” joins) we've found across the law code of Exodus:

The branch tree of Numbers 9

• performed the first Passover on time (9:2-3) → FULFILLED
• WHEN impure-by-corpse OR on-a-distant-journey (9:10) → DEFERRED to month 2, day 14, twilight, same statutes (9:11-12)
• pure AND present AND refusing (9:13 — all three words are in the verse) → CUT OFF
• anyone else who missed — e.g. the man who forgot: the penalty clause cannot reach him (he did not refuse), and the second Passover stands open → DEFERRED, still exposed if he refuses the second
• the stranger (9:14) → no branch at all: one statute for all.

STEP 3Write the code — from the ink only — and meet the gap

The gap that teaches the whole method: the verse says “on a distant journey” — בְדֶרֶךְ רְחֹקָה — and never says how far “distant” is. No number, no landmark. A careless coder would invent a number or copy one from the Mishnah into the source. Our law forbids both. The code below declares the threshold as an input parameter — a value the code accepts but does not contain. And the written scroll itself marks this exact word: in Numbers 9:10, the word רְחֹקָה (“distant”) carries a scribal dot over the letter heh — one of the Torah's rare dotted words — and the Mishnah's Rabbi Yosei reads that dot as the flag: not literally distant. The ink flags its own parameter.

Here is the entire code file, verbatim (it lives at passover_lesson.py in the workshop — run it yourself with python3 passover_lesson.py). Every line carries the verse that licenses it:

#!/usr/bin/env python3
# LESSON ONE — the Second Passover: the verse becomes code.
# THE LAW OF THIS FILE (owner-ruled 2026-09-03): the CODE below is
# built from the ink of Numbers 9 alone. The Mishnah appears ONLY in
# the DATA section at the bottom — test cases and parameter settings
# fed in at run time. Nothing from the Mishnah is inside the code.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# THE CODE — every line cites the verse word that licenses it
# ---------------------------------------------------------------

def passover(person, data):
    """Numbers 9:2-14 as a machine.
    `person` — the input case.  `data` — the values the ink does not
    state (here: which location counts as 'distant')."""

    # Numbers 9:14 — "one statute shall there be for you, and for the
    # stranger, and for the native of the land": the stranger is not a
    # branch; the same function runs for him.  (No code needed — the
    # absence of a branch IS the implementation.)

    # Numbers 9:10 — "any man WHEN he is impure for a corpse
    # [tamei la-nefesh] OR on a distant journey [ba-derekh rechokah]"
    deferred_class = person["impure_by_corpse"] or \
        is_distant(person["location"], data)

    if person["performed_first"]:
        # Numbers 9:2-3 — "the children of Israel shall perform the
        # Passover at its appointed time... on the fourteenth day of
        # this month at twilight... according to all its statutes"
        return "FULFILLED at the first Passover (month 1, day 14)"

    if deferred_class:
        # Numbers 9:11 — "in the SECOND month on the fourteenth day
        # at twilight they shall perform it; with unleavened bread
        # and bitter herbs they shall eat it"
        # Numbers 9:12 — "they shall leave none of it until morning,
        # nor break a bone of it: according to every statute of the
        # Passover they shall perform it"
        return ("DEFERRED to the second Passover (month 2, day 14, "
                "twilight; same statutes) — and EXEMPT from being cut "
                "off for the first")
        # the exemption rides the verse NAMING this class for
        # deferral rather than condemnation — see the Mishnah's own
        # question about why only these two are named (data file).

    if person["refused"]:
        # Numbers 9:13 — "the man who is PURE, and was NOT on a
        # journey, and REFRAINS [ve-chadal] from performing the
        # Passover — that soul shall be CUT OFF from its people"
        return "CUT OFF (karet) — pure, present, and refraining"

    # The remaining case: missed the first, but neither in the named
    # deferral class nor a refuser (the ink's karet clause requires
    # REFRAINING — chadal, a chosen desisting). The second Passover
    # stands open to him; refusing THAT would make him a refuser.
    return ("DEFERRED to the second Passover — and LIABLE to be cut "
            "off if he refrains from the second as well")


def is_distant(location, data):
    # Numbers 9:10 — the ink gives the PREDICATE ("on a distant
    # journey") but no number and no landmark. The threshold is DATA.
    scale = data["distance_scale"]          # ordered, near -> far
    return scale.index(location) > scale.index(data["distant_threshold"])


# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# THE DATA — from the Mishnah, fed at run time, never in the code
# ---------------------------------------------------------------

# Mishnah Pesachim 9:2 — the parameter's TWO recorded settings:
# "What is a distant journey? From Modiim and beyond — Rabbi Akiva.
#  Rabbi Eliezer says: from the threshold of the Temple courtyard
#  and beyond."  (A dispute = two data configurations. Both run.)
SCALE = ["courtyard", "jerusalem", "modiim", "beyond_modiim"]
SETTINGS = {
    "Rabbi Akiva":    {"distance_scale": SCALE, "distant_threshold": "modiim"},
    "Rabbi Eliezer":  {"distance_scale": SCALE, "distant_threshold": "courtyard"},
}

# Mishnah Pesachim 9:1 — the test cases and expected outputs:
# "One who was impure or on a distant journey and did not observe the
#  first — observes the second. One who forgot or was prevented and
#  did not observe the first — observes the second. If so, why does
#  the verse name the impure and the distant? That THESE are exempt
#  from karet, and THOSE are liable to karet [if they skip the
#  second]."
CASES = [
    ("the impure man (in Jerusalem)",
     {"impure_by_corpse": True, "location": "jerusalem",
      "performed_first": False, "refused": False},
     "DEFERRED", "EXEMPT"),
    ("the far traveler (beyond Modiim)",
     {"impure_by_corpse": False, "location": "beyond_modiim",
      "performed_first": False, "refused": False},
     "DEFERRED", "EXEMPT"),
    # REFINEMENT after the first run: these two must stand INSIDE the
    # courtyard, or the case is ambiguous between the two settings —
    # under Rabbi Eliezer, a man merely in Jerusalem is already in the
    # distant class. The machine caught the underspecified data; the
    # fix is in the DATA, never the code.
    ("the man who simply refused (pure, at the courtyard)",
     {"impure_by_corpse": False, "location": "courtyard",
      "performed_first": False, "refused": True},
     "CUT OFF", None),
    ("the man who forgot (pure, at the courtyard)",
     {"impure_by_corpse": False, "location": "courtyard",
      "performed_first": False, "refused": False},
     "DEFERRED", "LIABLE"),
    ("anyone who performed the first",
     {"impure_by_corpse": False, "location": "jerusalem",
      "performed_first": True, "refused": False},
     "FULFILLED", None),
]

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# THE TEST — feed the data in; require the Mishnah's output
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
if __name__ == "__main__":
    for setting_name, data in SETTINGS.items():
        print("== running under the %s setting (threshold: %s) ==" %
              (setting_name, data["distant_threshold"]))
        ok = 0
        for label, person, want, karet in CASES:
            out = passover(person, data)
            match = want in out and (karet is None or karet in out)
            ok += match
            print("  %-46s -> %s   [%s]" %
                  (label, out.split(" — ")[0], "ok" if match else "MISMATCH"))
        print("  %d/%d cases match the Mishnah's verdicts\n" % (ok, len(CASES)))

    # The dispute made visible: one case that CLASSIFIES DIFFERENTLY
    # under the two recorded settings — a pure man standing in
    # Jerusalem (inside Modiim, outside the courtyard) who missed the
    # first Passover without refusing:
    probe = {"impure_by_corpse": False, "location": "jerusalem",
             "performed_first": False, "refused": False}
    print("== the dispute as two runtime settings ==")
    for setting_name, data in SETTINGS.items():
        cls = "IN the deferred class" if is_distant(probe["location"], data) \
              else "NOT in the deferred class"
        print("  under %-14s the man in Jerusalem is %s" % (setting_name, cls))
    print("  (same code, same case — the recorded dispute is two DATA settings)")

STEP 4Now — and only now — read the entire Mishnah

The code is finished and the Mishnah never touched it. Here are the three Mishnah paragraphs of this law, complete, Hebrew and English, with what each one gives us as data:

Mishnah Pesachim 9:1 — the test cases and their expected outputs
מִי שֶׁהָיָה טָמֵא אוֹ בְדֶרֶךְ רְחוֹקָה וְלֹא עָשָׂה אֶת הָרִאשׁוֹן, יַעֲשֶׂה אֶת הַשֵּׁנִי. שָׁגַג אוֹ נֶאֱנַס וְלֹא עָשָׂה אֶת הָרִאשׁוֹן, יַעֲשֶׂה אֶת הַשֵּׁנִי. אִם כֵּן, לָמָּה נֶאֱמַר טָמֵא אוֹ שֶׁהָיָה בְדֶרֶךְ רְחוֹקָה, שֶׁאֵלּוּ פְּטוּרִין מֵהִכָּרֵת, וְאֵלּוּ חַיָּבִין בְּהִכָּרֵת
“One who was IMPURE or on a DISTANT JOURNEY and did not observe the first [Passover] — shall observe the second. One who FORGOT or was PREVENTED and did not observe the first — shall observe the second. IF SO, why does the verse say ‘impure or on a distant journey’? That THESE are exempt from being cut off, and THOSE are liable to being cut off [if they also miss the second].”
Read it as a test file. Row 1: impure → second (that's Numbers 9:10 straight). Row 2: forgot/prevented → second — a case the verse never names; where does it come from? From the ink's own precision: the cutting-off clause (9:13) fires only on the REFUSER (ve-chadal), so the forgetter falls outside it, and the second stands open to him. Row 3 is the teacher explaining WHY the verse names only two classes: to mark who is exempt from the penalty and who is still exposed. The Mishnah is not adding law — it is reading the else-branch's three conditions exactly.
Mishnah Pesachim 9:2 — the parameter, with TWO recorded settings
אֵיזוֹ הִיא דֶרֶךְ רְחוֹקָה, מִן הַמּוֹדִיעִים וְלַחוּץ, וּכְמִדָּתָהּ לְכָל רוּחַ, דִּבְרֵי רַבִּי עֲקִיבָא. רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר אוֹמֵר, מֵאַסְקֻפַּת הָעֲזָרָה וְלַחוּץ. אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹסֵי, לְפִיכָךְ נָקוּד עַל ה׳, לוֹמַר, לֹא מִפְּנֵי שֶׁרְחוֹקָה וַדַּאי, אֶלָּא מֵאִסְקֻפַּת הָעֲזָרָה וְלַחוּץ
“WHAT IS A DISTANT JOURNEY? From Modi’im and beyond, and the same distance in every direction — the words of Rabbi Akiva. Rabbi Eliezer says: from the THRESHOLD OF THE TEMPLE COURTYARD and beyond. Rabbi Yosei said: therefore there is A DOT OVER THE LETTER HEH [in the word רְחֹקָה, ‘distant’, Numbers 9:10] — to say: not because he is truly distant, but from the threshold of the courtyard and beyond.”
This is the DATA row. The code's predicate — ‘on a distant journey’ — needs a threshold the verse never gives as a number. Here are its two recorded settings: Rabbi Akiva sets it at Modi’im (a town about a day's walk out); Rabbi Eliezer sets it at the courtyard door. And Rabbi Yosei's reason is astonishing: in the written scroll, the word ‘distant’ carries a SCRIBAL DOT over one letter — the ink itself flags the word as qualified, and the teacher reads the flag. A dispute over a parameter is two configurations of the same code — so our test below runs BOTH.
Mishnah Pesachim 9:3 — more expected outputs: how the two runs differ
מַה בֵּין פֶּסַח רִאשׁוֹן לַשֵּׁנִי, הָרִאשׁוֹן אָסוּר בְּבַל יֵרָאֶה וּבַל יִמָּצֵא, וְהַשֵּׁנִי, מַצָּה וְחָמֵץ עִמּוֹ בַּבָּיִת. הָרִאשׁוֹן טָעוּן הַלֵּל בַּאֲכִילָתוֹ, וְהַשֵּׁנִי אֵינוֹ טָעוּן הַלֵּל בַּאֲכִילָתוֹ. זֶה וָזֶה טָעוּן הַלֵּל בַּעֲשִׂיָּתָן, וְנֶאֱכָלִין צָלִי עַל מַצּוֹת וּמְרוֹרִים, וְדוֹחִין אֶת הַשַּׁבָּת
“WHAT IS THE DIFFERENCE between the first Passover and the second? The first is bound by ‘it shall not be seen’ and ‘it shall not be found’ [the leaven bans], while on the second, unleavened and leavened bread are with him in the house. The first requires hallel [psalms of praise] as it is eaten; the second does not. BOTH require hallel as they are prepared, and are eaten roasted, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, and BOTH override the Sabbath.”
A differences table — expected outputs for a richer statute-set than our small lesson codes. Notice what it confirms about the ink: Numbers 9:11-12 attached the NIGHT statutes (‘with unleavened bread and bitter herbs... every statute of the Passover’) to the second — and the Mishnah's shared column (roasted, matzah, bitter herbs, overrides the Sabbath) is exactly that attachment, while the differences (leaven in the house, hallel at eating) track statutes the deferral verses did NOT import. Test data for the next refinement of this lesson.
