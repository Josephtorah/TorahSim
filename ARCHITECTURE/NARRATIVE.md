# NARRATIVE — the paragraphs that open under the summary's bullets

One heading per block, the block's bullets as they stand in the summary, and under each bullet an indented
paragraph of three to five sentences. The paragraph keeps one shape so the page teaches by rhythm: what happens
in the text; what the machine does with it; what the tradition adds, with its source named; what carries forward.

## Bereshit (in the beginning) — Genesis 1:1-6:8

- Boots the world in seven ledger days: a clock, two containers (heaven and earth), light, sky, land and seas, plants, the lights, the creatures, humanity. Each day is commanded, built, tested, named, and committed. Day seven's transaction is left open.
  In seven days the text speaks the world into being, and the machine reads each day as one transaction: a command issued, a build, a receipt ('and it was so'), a test ('God saw that it was good'), a name, and a commit ('evening and morning'). Day two never runs the test, and the machine records that emptiness as the finding. Day seven has no evening-and-morning line at all, so the week ends with a transaction still open, and the first holiness status in the corpus is written to a day rather than a thing (2:3). The tradition counts the speech acts as ten utterances (Avot 5:1, enumerated at Bereshit Rabbah 17:1), counting 1:1 as the first although no 'and He said' stands there. Three things carry forward: the evening-first day cycle exported at 1:5, which becomes the Sabbath's clock and the evening-first order of the Shema; the work-word of 2:2, which becomes the Sabbath law's operative term at Exodus 20:10; and the week itself, which the tradition later reads the tabernacle as rebuilding day by day (Tanchuma, Pekudei).

- Installs the first rule (the tree) and traces the first violation: the rule misquoted, the eating, the sentences, the exile.
  Eden gives the man a job and one rule: eat from every tree, not from the tree of knowledge, with the penalty 'in the day you eat' (2:16-17). The machine installs it as the corpus's first prohibition with a standing penalty handler, then watches the rule get quoted three times, by the serpent, the woman, and the serpent again, with seven mutations against the frozen text, including a clause the rule never had, 'nor touch it'. The eating fires the handler; the interrogation ('where are you', 'who told you') is recorded as a testimony chain with two admissions; and the sentences are written as the corpus's first curses, on the serpent and on the ground, never on the man, with mortality as a boundary ('until you return to dust') rather than a same-day death. The tradition reads the sentencing order, serpent then woman then man, as a rule of its own: in punishment one begins from the lesser (Bereshit Rabbah on 3:14). The touch-word invented inside the misquotation begins a long career: it is the purity law's contact mechanism in Leviticus.

- Records the first offerings, the first murder and its sentence, the first city and crafts, and the death ledger of Adam's line down to Noah.
  Worship begins uninstructed. Cain and Abel bring offerings in the vocabulary Leviticus will later regulate (the grain gift, firstlings, fat), and one is regarded. The machine reads 4:7 as the corpus's first 'if', two branches installed as handlers, if you do well and if you do not, with 'sin at the door' inheriting the exact grammar of the woman's sentence at 3:16; then the first death is a murder, followed by the lie, the ground's mouth, a sentence of wandering, a protective mark, and a sevenfold handler for anyone who kills Cain. Cain's line founds the first city and the crafts (tents, music, metal), and Lamech's boast inflates the divine decree to seventy-seven, a quotation the machine diffs against its source. Adam's line is then a death ledger: nine totals, each row closing 'and he died', except the seventh, where the verbs change to 'walked' and 'was not'. The tradition reads that book as a registry already written and shown to the first man with every generation in it (Bereshit Rabbah on 5:1), and the Mishnah later proves the duty of procreation from 5:2's 'male and female'.

- Opens the flood: the stolen verdict, the hundred and twenty years, the decree.
  The prologue runs the week's inspection formula again with creatures as the subject: the sons of God see that the daughters are good, and take. The machine records the second divine time-decree, the hundred and twenty years, with its cruxes left open (a cap on life, or a countdown to the flood), and the inspection verb returning to God with the verdict flipped: 1:31's 'very good' answered by 'great evil', the book's second difference between what was ordered and what was delivered. The wipe scope walks the creation list backward, and five words of favor stand against it: Noah. The tradition finds Moses in this verse in cipher, the letters of 'inasmuch' totaling the letters of his name, and the Mishnah rules from 'My spirit shall not abide' that the flood generation has no share and does not stand in judgment (Sanhedrin 10:3). Nothing is built in these eight verses. The ark is the next block's specification.

## Noach (Noah) — Genesis 6:9-11:32

- Takes the ark as a specification (dimensions, decks, door), then runs it: the boarding with its dates, the rise, the remembering, the waters going home, the exit and the altar.
  The ark is given as a specification, dimensions and decks and a door, and Noah builds it: the corpus's second human-built thing, and the first receipted complete, where Cain's city was a participle that never finished. The machine reads the span as its first fully settled command cycle, two divine imperatives pushed and both popped by one line of compliance, 'and Noah did all that God commanded him.' Then the run: the boarding dated to the day, the register's first real calendar value, year six hundred, month two, day seventeen, epoched on one man's life; forty days and a hundred and fifty as durations; the remembering, the waters going home, the exit, and an altar built unbidden, the corpus's third built thing. The tradition reads the seven days' delay before the rain as mourning for Methuselah, a righteous man's merit suspending the punishment (Bereshit Rabbah on 7:10). The come-command of 7:1 is answered by the go-out command of 8:16, the same pair the exodus will use at national scale.

- Installs the blood law with the blessing: be fruitful again, but no blood, and life for life. The first standing law binding all humanity.
  After the flood the blessing of the first week is re-issued to a plural target, Noah and his sons, two verbs shorter than 1:28: be fruitful, multiply, fill, with subdue and rule gone and fear put in their place. The machine installs the span's laws as standing facts: the eating grant amended to include the live-mover class, and the corpus's second prohibition, no blood, encoded on the exact pattern of the garden's first, both of them eating laws. Life for life is written as the first standing sanction binding all humanity. The tradition asks why a chapter that can never govern a future case is kept at all, since the oath forecloses another flood, and answers that it is kept for its laws (Bereshit Rabbah on 9:1); it also reads 'as the green herb' as forbidding theft, a prohibition hidden inside a permission. The fear laid on the beasts here is the word Deuteronomy later lays on the nations.

- Sets the covenant of the bow as a standing handler: the bow in the cloud, and God remembers.
  The covenant is established in three grammatical steps, a participle, a future chain, and a closing perfect, with the covenant-word appearing seven times in ten verses. The machine installs its heart as a handler, the same operator the law will use: if I bring clouds and the bow is seen, then I remember My covenant. It notes that the verb that will seal every later covenant, 'to cut,' has its first token here negated, 'all flesh shall not be cut off,' and that every later bow in the Torah is a weapon; only this one is a sign. The tradition counts the rainbow among the ten things created at twilight before the first Sabbath, so the bow here is placed, not made (Bereshit Rabbah on 9:13), and reads 'for generations' written short as marking the generations that needed no sign. The handler stands armed for the rest of the corpus.

- Records the vineyard curse, the table of nations, Babel's build and its halt, and Shem's ledger down to Terach.
  Noah plants a vineyard on the garden's own verb, the Torah's second planter after God, and the first human curse and the first human blessing come from one mouth in adjacent verses. The nations table installs four cities by its one builder and gives the word 'nation' its first six tokens, the seventh being 12:2's great nation. Babel is the corpus's first abandoned project: the builders' 'let us make' is the very verb of 1:26, four human demands stand open forever over a cessation, and God's 'let Us confuse' is popped by 'He confused' in the same root. Shem's ledger drops the death column from Adam's template and lets death back in twice, both times deviant, a son dying before his father at 11:28. The tradition counts this ledger as the ten generations from Noah to Abraham (Avot 5:2) and computes from 11:29 that Haran begot at six, a number it pleads as evidence in court.

## Lech Lecha (go forth) — Genesis 12:1-17:27

- Records the call and the journey, and opens the land and seed promises as demands on the ledger. They stay open through the whole book.
  The call withholds its object, 'the land that I will show you,' and the machine records the corpus's first command with the destination deleted; the tradition reads the concealment as a payment mechanism, reward per stride (Bereshit Rabbah on 12:1). Abram goes, and the receipt carries the letter's countersignature, 'as the LORD had spoken to him.' The promises of land and seed are opened as demands on the ledger, and 13:15's 'forever' is the corpus's first perpetuity formula in a land pledge, held as promise-fact and not yet as invariant. They stay open through the whole book, and the world hash carries them. The tradition maps 12:2's clauses onto the opening blessing of the daily prayer one for one, and reads 12:3's 'I will bless those who bless you' as live legal authority for the priestly blessing.

- Records the descent to Egypt, the separation from Lot, the war of the kings, and the priest's blessing.
  Abram's first recorded speech in the corpus is spoken at a border, out of fear: the sister-word. The machine records the descent to Egypt with three human demands and no divine one, and the tradition reads the episode as a specification the nation later executes, eleven matched word-pairs between this descent and Israel's (Bereshit Rabbah on 12:10). The separation from Lot at the Jordan is the corpus's first fulfilled human demand, 'separate from me,' and the quarrel that caused it is the verse two law tractates open with, 'nothing good comes from quarrel.' The war of the kings is eleven verses of annal with no speech, the word 'king' born twenty-seven times, and it ends with a refusal: Abram takes nothing from Sodom's king. A priest appears for the first time, and he is a Canaanite king of Salem.

- Runs the covenant of the pieces (the stars, the four hundred years) and Hagar's angel; installs the law of the flesh (circumcision) and the renamings of Abraham and Sarah.
  The covenant of the pieces opens with Abram's first words to God, 'what will You give me,' and closes the give-arc in the perfect: the land is given. Between them stand the stars, the four-hundred-year decree as a standing fact in the future chain, and the rite of the halved animals, which the prophets later name as the form a covenant takes (Jeremiah 34, read at this seat). Hagar's chapter puts the first female demand-issuer in the corpus and lets a slave woman name God, the registry's boldest write. Then the law of the flesh: circumcision installed as an everlasting covenant, the renamings of Abraham and Sarah written as decrees without the naming formula, and the laugh born face-down in the father's doubt. The tradition counts thirteen covenants cut over circumcision in this chapter's own tokens (Mishnah Nedarim 3:11), a census the machine checked.

## Vayera (and He appeared) — Genesis 18:1-22:24

- Records the visitors at Mamre, the plea for Sodom with its bargaining count, the overthrow, the pillar, the cave.
  Three men arrive at Mamre, and the machine keeps the letter's count, three men, never angels in this span. The largest chapter of Genesis pushes seven demands and every one is Abraham's, none divine, and none pops: a morsel of bread promised becomes curds, milk, and a calf delivered, a difference the letter itself records. Sarah's laugh is the first female interior speech in the corpus. The plea for Sodom gives birth to the court's vocabulary, 'judgment' twice, 'draw near,' 'answer,' as Abraham bargains the count down from fifty to ten. The tradition measures 'the heat of the day' at six hours by a physical test against the manna's fourth hour (Bereshit Rabbah on 18:1).

- Runs the Gerar dream-court: the prophet's prayer, the shut wombs; then Isaac's birth, the expulsion, and the well-oath.
  Sodom's overthrow is the corpus's first violated prohibition: 'do not look back' is pushed at 19:17 and breached at 19:26, and the pillar is the ledger's record of the breach. The tradition reads the city's practice as legislation, a statute enacted by their predecessors and enforced even against Abraham's household (Bereshit Rabbah on 19:5). Gerar runs a dream-court: God speaks to a foreign king at night, and the machine records the words 'prophet' and 'pray' entering the corpus here, Abraham named an intercessor by God to a Philistine. Isaac's birth executes the decree of 17:19 as the registry's one write, the expulsion sends Hagar's weeping into the wilderness, and the well-oath at Beersheba is the swear-verb's whole debut field in one settled cycle. Sarah's 'she rises with him' becomes a standing rule of marriage law at this verse.

- Records the binding as a test and closes it with the oath: the seed as stars, the gate of enemies.
  The binding is the second consecutive chapter in which the week's instruments fall silent: no 'good,' no 'righteous,' no receipt fires, and the machine marks it the first unmarked clock since Mamre, everything paid on the letter's terms and nothing on the machine's. The compound command, take, go, offer, has a fourth token that is God's own. The verb 'bind' occurs once in the whole Torah, here. The tradition disputes what kind of thing this is, exaltation like a banner raised, or a literal test 'so people should not say He stunned him' (Bereshit Rabbah on 22:1), and it takes Abraham's early rising as the source of the rule that the zealous are early to commandments (Pesachim 4a). The oath that closes it is the swear-verb's divine turn, 'by Myself,' and the seed-as-stars promise is what the block hands forward.

## Chayei Sarah (the life of Sarah) — Genesis 23:1-25:18

- Records the Machpelah purchase as a complete transaction: price weighed, witnesses at the gate, the deed's language. The tradition's source for acquisition by money.
  Sarah's years are written as three clauses, a hundred year and twenty year and seven years, and the tradition reads each against the next: at a hundred like twenty, at twenty like seven. The purchase is the corpus's fullest transaction: Efron's price named in the hearing of the gate, the silver weighed, the field, the cave, and the trees deeded together, and three frozen promises land in it, the bury-verb's first token being 15:15's 'you shall be buried.' The machine records the weigh-verb's debut with a two-token career, this payment and the bride-price of Exodus 22:16. The man who holds the whole land's promise introduces himself with the decree's own word, a stranger and sojourner, and acquires a grave. Kiddushin 2a later reads the field of Ephron as the source of acquisition by money.

- Runs the servant's oath and the well test, the retelling, the release, and the betrothal; then Abraham's end and Ishmael's line.
  The servant is put under oath and sets a test at the well, water for the man and for the camels unasked, and Rebekah passes it before he has finished speaking. The retelling at Laban's table repeats the day with the letter's own variations, the release is sought and granted, and the betrothal is transacted with gifts and a blessing. The tradition reads Laban and Bethuel's 'from the LORD the matter has come' as unknowing witness to a decree issued at Moriah (Bereshit Rabbah on 24:50), a thread the machine records in the previous block. Abraham's end closes his ledger with the burial by both sons, and Ishmael's line is counted as twelve princes. The idiom 'to the right or to the left' is cashed against the family map: the right Ishmael, the left Lot.

## Toledot (generations) — Genesis 25:19-28:9

- Records the twins and the birthright sale; Isaac in Gerar with the sister-claim, the hundredfold, the wells, and the covenant swear.
  Isaac's generations open with twenty years of barrenness and a prayer the tradition reads two ways, poured out abundantly or a decree overturned like grain under a pitchfork (Bereshit Rabbah on 25:21), and a struggle in the womb read as two legal systems colliding, each permitting what the other forbids. The twins are born and the birthright is sold for a meal, a transaction sealed with an oath. In Gerar the sister-claim runs a third time and the machine holds the episode pair across its walls; the tradition imports sanctuary law to bind Isaac to the land, an unblemished offering that leaves the curtains is disqualified (Bereshit Rabbah on 26:2). The hundredfold harvest, the wells renamed, the covenant sworn with Abimelech, and the well called Shibah close the span. The tradition reads the well-names as the table of contents of the Torah itself.

- Records the hunt-command and the blessing delivered to the wrong party. The machine flags it: the demand's addressee and its performer do not match.
  Isaac's command to Esau, hunt and bring, and the blessing delivered to Jacob are read by the machine as a demandee mismatch: the demand's addressee and its performer do not match, and the machine flags it rather than resolving it. The tradition supplies four causes for the blind eyes that made it possible and drops none, among them the angels' tears at the binding falling into them (Bereshit Rabbah on 27:1), a wire back to the previous block. A numeric rule is seated on 27:2, within five years of a parent's age one begins to fear death, with the arithmetic supplied, a hundred and twenty-three against the mother's hundred and twenty-seven. Esau's cry and the second, lesser blessing are recorded as spoken. Nothing in the span is repaired; the mismatch stands in the ledger.

- Records Esau's grudge, the flight order, and the sending to Paddan.
  Esau's grudge is an interior monologue the tradition reconstructs as a complete plan, kill the father first, then the brother, inherit alone (Bereshit Rabbah on 27:41), and it reads the same waiting for the days of mourning as a merit as well. Rebekah's pressure produces the flight order, and Isaac sends Jacob to Paddan with the blessing of Abraham restated. Esau, hearing, takes a third wife from Ishmael's house. The machine records the block as a set of human commands, go, take, and their execution, with the divine promise carried in Isaac's mouth rather than God's. The road to Haran is the next block's opening.

## Vayetze (and he went out) — Genesis 28:10-32:3

- Records the Bethel ladder, the name written on the place, and the first vow, opened as a demand.
  Jacob sleeps at a place the machine treats as unnamed until he names it: the ladder, the angels, the promise repeated in Jacob's own hearing, and the first vow in the corpus, opened as a demand with conditions, if God will be with me. The tradition states the grammar rule this machine runs, that a word wanting the preposition 'to' may take a letter at its end instead, with this verse among its examples (Yevamot 13b), and counts the stones three ways, twelve, three, two, each a different test. Bethel is written into the registry. The vow stands open for seven chapters, and the audit arrives at 35:1.

- Runs the wage of seven years and the switched bride; the opened wombs and the twelve name-writes with their reasons; the speckled-wage contract and the peeled rods.
  At the well the great stone is rolled away and Rachel is met; the tradition binds this one frame, a well, three flocks, a stone, a gathering, to six institutions and says so outright (Bereshit Rabbah on 29:2). The wage is set at seven years and the bride is switched, and the tradition reads the drafting as verse-perfect, three deliberate terms against three named swaps, with the fraud going around the words rather than through them. The wombs open and twelve names are written with their reasons, the tradition classifying names against deeds rather than listing them. The speckled wage is a second contract and the peeled rods are how it was performed; the tradition reads 'I have divined' as a household audit, and Onkelos writes 'I have tested.' Judah's name fixes a threshold for thanks at the fourth son, beyond her share.

- Records the return-command, the flight over the river, the pursuit and search, and the heap of witness named in two tongues.
  The return command comes with a condition the tradition reads as law, possessions outside the land carry no blessing, so the promise rides on the return itself (Bereshit Rabbah on 31:3). Jacob takes counsel in the open field, and the rule about secrets is drawn from where they stood. Laban pursues, is warned at night in the truncated speech the tradition assigns to the nations' prophets, and searches every tent; the machine records the quarrel as containing no blows, only appeasement. The heap of witness is named in two tongues, and the tradition finds a legal age definition in Jacob's protest: a day-old ram is called a ram (Bava Kamma 65b). The covenant at the heap, a boundary and an oath, closes the household ledger.

## Vayishlach (and he sent) — Genesis 32:4-36:43

- Records the two camps and the gift, the wrestled name (Israel written), the blessing returned to Esau, and the first altar.
  Jacob divides into two camps and sends a gift ahead, a prudence the tradition turns into a rule, do not put all your wealth in one corner (Bereshit Rabbah on 32:8), while it audits the patriarch for calling himself Esau's servant and counts eight kings as the price. The wrestling by night writes Israel into the registry and marks the sinew. The meeting with Esau returns the blessing with the word itself, and at Shechem the block's first altar is built and a field is bought, the second land purchase of the book. The dotted word at 33:4 is the exact tie case of the dotting rule, six letters and six dots, expound both. Grace is heard for eleven sons and the twelfth is deferred ten chapters.

- Records the deceit at the gate: nine demands made, none performed. Then Bethel again, three deaths on the road, and Esau's ledger of chiefs and kings.
  Dinah's chapter is the corpus's largest set of unperformed demands: nine made at the gate, none kept, and the machine records the deceit as the letter records it. The tradition draws the words for divine love from the assailant's own clauses and says so (Bereshit Rabbah on 34:3), while Onkelos renders the brothers' answer as given with wisdom. Bethel again executes the vow's audit; the tradition reads the command to arise as the writ that scrutiny follows delay (Bereshit Rabbah on 35:1). Three deaths fall on the road, Deborah, Rachel at Bethlehem, Isaac at Mamre, and the registry writes Israel a second time. Esau's chapter is a ledger of chiefs and kings the tradition peels like an onion to expose the births it hides.

## Vayeshev (and he settled) — Genesis 37:1-40:23

- Records the dreamer sent, stripped, and sold.
  Joseph's report opens a ledger the next two blocks close: the tradition repays his three reports one for one on the scales of Proverbs, the charge of eating from the living repaid by the slaughtered goat (Bereshit Rabbah on 37:2). The sheaf-noun stands in the whole Torah only in this verse. The brothers strip him, sell him, and stain the coat, and the machine records the sale as an event and the father's mourning as a state left standing. The received translation decides the reason for the love, a wise son rather than a son of old age. The caravan takes him to Egypt, and the ledger opened here stays open until the cup.

- Records Judah and Tamar: the pledge taken as evidence, the levirate duty, the verdict reversed by the pledge itself.
  Judah takes a Canaanite wife and his sons die; the tradition reads Judah as the first to perform the levirate duty, generations before Deuteronomy commands it (Bereshit Rabbah on 38:8). Tamar, denied, takes a pledge, a signet, a cord, a staff, against a promised kid, and when the sentence of burning is spoken the pledge speaks and the verdict is reversed. The machine records the pledge as evidence and the kid as the price, and finds the kid of goats at exactly four places in Genesis, in two matched pairs, the deceiving of Isaac and this. Perez and Zerah are born with the scarlet thread. Judah's 'she is more right than I' is the verdict the block carries.

- Records Potiphar's house (the refusal, the false charge) and the two dreams in prison, with the petition that is forgotten.
  In Potiphar's house the formula 'the LORD was with Joseph' stands twice before the fall and twice after, framing the chapter. The refusal is recorded as speech, and the tradition reads the master's anger as directed at the story rather than the man, 'I know you did not do it,' with the committal read as an appointment (Bereshit Rabbah on 39:19). In prison two dreams are interpreted, and the machine records Joseph's petition, remember me, mention me, as a demand pushed and forgotten. The tradition prices the two words at two years, one added year of imprisonment for each (Bereshit Rabbah on 40:14). The delay is a term, not an oversight.

## Miketz (at the end) — Genesis 41:1-44:17

- Records Pharaoh's dreams and Joseph's rise: the seven-year plan, the storing, the selling.
  Pharaoh's dreams arrive at the end of two full years, and the tradition reads the delay as a term set and kept. The interpreters fail, and their failed reading is preserved verbatim, seven daughters born and seven buried. Joseph reads the dreams as one, proposes the seven-year plan, and rises; the machine records the storing and the selling as the famine's state machine, plenty, then dearth, then Egypt selling grain to the world. The chapter's spelling of the troubled spirit is contrasted letter by letter with Daniel's, and the machine checked the contrast. The rise sets the frame for the descent.

- Records the first descent (the brothers before the governor, the money returned in the sacks) and the second (Benjamin brought down, the meal, the cup planted).
  The brothers go down for grain, and Joseph's command to buy is read by letter-value as two hundred and ten, the years of the servitude the errand begins (Bereshit Rabbah on 42:2). The tradition also seats the fast-day rule on 42:1, whoever forgot and ate should not display it before the community. Simeon is held, the money returns in the sacks, and the second descent brings Benjamin on Judah's surety, which the tradition later reads as the founding verse of surety law, 'I will be surety for him' (Bava Batra 173b). The meal, the fivefold portion, and the cup planted in Benjamin's sack close the block with the trap set. The cup's word is the word the Torah elsewhere uses to outlaw divination, and Onkelos renders it as inquiry at both seats.

## Vayigash (and he drew near) — Genesis 44:18-47:27

- Records Judah's surety speech, the unmasking, and the brothers' guilt resolved.
  Judah's speech is the longest plea in Genesis, and the machine records the seam the previous unit left open, broken off mid-speech by design, closed at 'Joseph could not restrain himself.' The tradition finds here the Torah's own first example of the argument from the lesser to the greater, one of ten it counts in Scripture (Bereshit Rabbah on 44:8), the rule this machine cites everywhere. The unmasking is proved on the body, and the brothers' guilt is resolved by the man they wronged. The tradition mints the judgment-day argument on 45:3: if the youngest of the tribes rebuked them and they could not answer, how much more the rebuke of Heaven. The wagons and the garments are sent, and Jacob's spirit revives.

- Records the descent of the seventy, Goshen, and the fifth: Joseph's economy over Egypt, land and persons.
  The descent of the seventy is counted by a rule read off the roster's own phrasing, sons' daughters count like sons and daughters' sons do not (Bereshit Rabbah on 46:7), and the count in the next verses runs on it. The timber Abraham planted is cut here and carried to Egypt, the tradition tracking it across three generations into the boards of the tabernacle. Goshen is granted; the five brothers presented are read as the weak, shown so Pharaoh would not conscript the strong. Joseph's economy takes Egypt's money, cattle, land, and persons for grain, and the fifth is installed as a standing charge. Jacob's coming down in his son's hand is read as a punishment commuted, chains exchanged for a summons.

## Vayechi (and he lived) — Genesis 47:28-50:26

- Records the crossed hands (the blessing routed past the elder), the testament of the twelve, and the coffin in Egypt.
  Jacob crosses his hands over Ephraim and Manasseh, routing the blessing past the elder, and quotes the old promise back as the warrant for adopting them as tribes; the tradition reads 'an assembly of nations' as Manasseh and Ephraim, and the inheritance sugyot, the Talmud's discussions, seat the definition of a congregation on this verse. The testament of the twelve is blessing rather than chronology, because the End he came to reveal was concealed from him, and the tradition dates the whispered line of the liturgy to this scene: the sons say Hear, Israel, and he answers blessed be the Name (Pesachim 56a). The machine records twelve verdicts, one per son, and the burial command. The block closes Genesis's population.

- Leaves one oath open for Exodus to close: the bones to be carried up.
  Joseph mourns, embalms, and carries his father to Machpelah, and the tradition asks why Joseph died before his brothers, answering from the embalming or from the times he heard 'your servant my father' and said nothing (Bereshit Rabbah on 50:1). The brothers' fear is answered, and Joseph makes the sons of Israel swear: carry up my bones. The machine leaves that oath open at the book's last verse, a coffin in Egypt, and Exodus 13:19 closes it. The tradition and the machine agree on their own legs that no place called a threshing floor of brambles exists in Scripture. Genesis ends with the world populated and its ledger open.

## Shemot (names) — Exodus 1:1-6:1

- Records the names, the midwives (the first open demands of the book), the child drawn from the water, the bush and the Name, the signs and the firstborn, and bricks without straw.
  The names are counted again, and the midwives refuse Pharaoh's order; the machine records their refusal as the first open demands of the book, a king's command pushed and not performed. Moses is drawn from the water; the tradition computes from his sister's waiting an hour the seven days Israel later waited for her (Mishnah Sotah 1:9), and reads the killing of the Egyptian as the source of two laws about a gentile who strikes an Israelite (Sanhedrin 58b). The bush gives the Name, and the tradition files its ink-protection law here, the Name that may not be erased (Shevuot 35a). The signs and the firstborn threat are issued; at the lodging place circumcision is read as so great that righteous Moses was not suspended over it a full hour (Mishnah Nedarim 3:11). Bricks without straw closes with Pharaoh's 'who is the LORD,' which the tradition turns into a scribal rule about dating documents by kings.
