# ARCHITECTURE — the map of the compiled code, Genesis through Numbers

Written 2026-09-05 from the files as they stood that day (the Emor
sweep, Leviticus 21 to 24, was in flight and uncommitted; the exam stood
at round 45). Brought current 2026-09-13, at the close of the book of
Numbers: between 2026-09-09 and 2026-09-13 the fourth book was walked
from its first verse to its last, one weekly portion at a time, each
read on its shelf, frozen, compiled and put on the tape (the walk's map
is World/step9/NUMBERS_WALK.md, one section per sitting). The four
tutorials in this folder — THE_TOUR, THE_EFFECTS, THE_CLOCK with TIME,
THE_LINKS — carry the fourth book since the same day. This folder
describes the code; it is not the code. Nothing in it is canon, and
nothing outside this folder was changed to make it.

## How data moves

**Ink to Spec (Steps 2 and 3 of THE_STEPS).** A span is read under its
spine (Bereshit Rabbah for Genesis, the Mekhilta for Exodus, the Sifra
for Leviticus, the Sifrei on Numbers for Numbers — its sections found by
position, never by the export's typed heads — and Onkelos everywhere),
claims are extracted, the unit is written, gated, stamped, and frozen.
Genesis units carry narrative claims: who did what, what was promised,
what stands open. Exodus and Leviticus units carry law claims. Numbers
carries both, and its walk fixed the order of work: one portion is read
and frozen, then compiled and put on the tape, before the next is opened.

**Spec to World (the fold).** `corpus_world.py` folds the frozen units
in canonical order into one world. The hash has not moved since
2026-08-10. Note what the counts say: facts, events, and demands are the
same numbers the Genesis-only world reported. The law units of Exodus,
Leviticus and Numbers add standing law, 2,163 entries, and no narrative
facts. The world hash tracks the narrative.

**Ink to Code (motion 1 of Step 5).** Each `cold_run_*.py` reads the
bare tokens of its span from the word database, probes every token it
claims before running (the zero-report law), and writes its functions
with every ink-borne cell tagged INK. Quantities the ink does not state
become parameters, never constants. The fourth book taught the number
reader a grammar of its own — twenty-nine rules, each written first as a
probe that failed, then checked by a diff of the old reader against the
new over every verse of the Bible, read by hand.

**Test to Code (motions 2 to 5).** The Mishnah's rows are collected as
test data. The run prints every miss. Each miss sends the compiler to
the Talmud or the Sifra for that exact gap, and the recorded argument is
replayed as a labeled move on the changed line. When the matrix matches,
every cell carries its provenance: INK, a named MOVE, DATA (a
transmitted quantity), FENCE (a rabbinic boundary the answer sheet
labels as such), or IMPORT and ROUTED (a rule reached from another span
or book). A guard refuses any expectation not typed from the answer
sheet.

**Code to Spec (the findings loop).** What the compile discovers about
the ink is seated back into the frozen unit as a finding, with the
manifest, rendering, and standing count rebaked. Standing rises by
exactly one per seat; the hash does not move.

**Code and World to Runtime.** A compiled function becomes a daemon:
`law(event, world)` returning effects in the fixed interface
`{effect, subject, counterparty, amount, due, source_law, case_source}`.
Every daemon declares the verse that speaks its law and the act that
switches it on; the engine fires every daemon in force on every event,
writes effects to entity ledgers, sets timers for effects owed to the
future, cancels timers when a later text event voids them, and closes
open entries when the text narrates the doing. The clock moves only on a
date the text states. Every run writes a hash-chained journal; an index
built from it answers who wrote each ledger line, and five views (the
ledger, the timers, the clock, the docket, the population) are checked
against the engine's own tables by the journal gate. A cursor replays
the tape to a verse's left edge with the journal as the audit, and a
scenario is a case put to the live world there. At each checkpoint the
diff engine compares the computed ledger to what the text or the answer
sheet declares, and prints MATCH or DIVERGE.

## The invariants

- **Evidence immutable, models rewritable.** Units, ledgers, and tests
  are evidence. Layers 3 to 5 are models and may be rewritten freely
  while the gates stay green.
- **Code from the ink alone.** No Mishnah or Talmud row is encoded in a
  function. They enter as test data at run time.
- **The fence.** The engine never invents an event. Acts come from the
  text; the law computes only consequences and writes only the ledger.
- **Effects on every verdict.** Every compiled cell either writes a
  registered effect or states an honest no-change.
- **The link review law.** Every link between spans is a reference the
  text itself makes, or a transfer that names its teacher in the
  tradition; a transfer without a teacher is a labeled hypothesis,
  never counted.
- **Every measured claim from a print.** A count typed by hand is never
  the record's until the instrument has said it; the gates' indexes are
  regenerated, never edited.
- **The register gate.** The text's own counts, receipts, footers and
  register headers are read off the database and checked against the
  ledger and the population table before every compile sitting closes.
- **One derivation stream.** One session writes; the state doc records
  every step.
- **Hebrew never without English.** Every Hebrew token in every layer
  carries its gloss.

## What the two machines are

Layers 1 and 2 are the **proof machine**. The text is the program, it
has exactly one execution, and replaying it to the stamped hash is a
proof. It takes no inputs by design.

Layers 3, 4, and 5 are the **simulation machine**. Layer 3 is the
compiled law. Layer 4 grades it. Layer 5 runs it over entities and
time. The population it runs on is layer 2, the entities and events the
narrative supplies, and the closing acts (the ram brought, the iron
recovered, the census taken, the high priest dead) that come from the
text and close ledger entries the law opened. Since 2026-09-09 the
simulation machine has memory: the run halts on a case no law decides,
the text's answer installs a rule, and the next such case is decided by
the library.

## What is not here yet

- CHRONICLE.md, the screen for watching the program run, is a design
  the fourth book has not touched, by the owner's word. The seven
  machines of the fourth book in STATE_MACHINES.md have no drawings
  yet, and `diagrams/02_dependencies.svg` still shows the fourteen
  spans of 2026-09-05.
- Deuteronomy: 41 unit files, none frozen, no runner. The fourth book
  filed its forward seats — 4:41-43 and 19:1-13 for the refuge law's
  twin, 21:1-9 for the heifer, 17:6 and 19:15 for the witnesses, 25:2,
  12:1-2 — to be answered at the reading.
- The readback: the debts the fourth book leaves open by design are
  closed in Joshua (the Levites' cities, the six cities of refuge, the
  daughters' holding, Caleb's Hebron, the stipulation of Gad and
  Reuben) and one term in Joshua 24:33; the design for running those
  books' acts against the open ledger is owed at step 6 of the loop.
- Eight of the fourth book's checkpoints stand as declared divergences
  the shelf explains and the ink does not: the inclusive count twice,
  Miriam's day, the ten trials, Korach's death, the Sifrei's thirteen
  utterances, its ten descents against the ink's eleven, and the
  seventy's missing one.
- The narrative event typer, which would turn a narrative passage into an
  engine event by its opener lemma, does not exist. Every engine scene is
  hand-written from a recorded case.
- Genesis has no compiled law functions, but its story is whole on the
  engine: four runners carry Genesis 1:1 to 50:26 as events, and the
  story's verses feed the law's own rules at two seats (37:31, 44:12).
