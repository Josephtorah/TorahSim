## 42. Matot (tribes). Numbers 30:2-32:42

### Installs the vows: a man bound by his own word; a daughter's or a wife's vow confirmed by silence on the day of hearing or annulled that day, the hearing day the engine's own timer, and the late annulment charged to the husband.

Moses speaks to the heads of the tribes with no divine frame: a man who vows shall not profane his word; a daughter's vow in her father's house stands if he is silent on the day he hears and falls if he restrains her, and the LORD forgives her; a wife's likewise by her husband; a widow's stands; and he who annuls after having heard bears her iniquity. The machine writes the vow as a debit toward Heaven, runs the hearing day as a timer to the day's end, cancels it and closes the debt on a restraint by the right authority, and charges the late annulment to the husband; the wrong authority, the partial annulment and the messenger write nothing. The Sifrei reads the day two ways, to nightfall or twenty-four hours, and the machine keeps the second as the row's other arm; two of the Talmud's questions stand as rows marked unresolved. ### Records the war on Midian as the run of three open debts: Balaam and the five kings slain, the vessels purified by the tradition's analogy with Leviticus 11, the spoil halved and tributed one in five hundred and one in fifty, the officers' gold in the half-shekel's own words.

The sentence on the captives stands open forever, a command with no narrated run. The parser was taught here to read "one of five hundred" as a rate and never to count its denominator.

### Records Gad and Reuben's request, Moses' rebuke and the doubled condition, the tradition's source for the law of every condition, the grant given from now with the crossing left open to Joshua, the cities built, and Machir, Jair and Nobah.

The sons of Gad and Reuben, rich in cattle, ask for the land of Jazer and Gilead; Moses answers with the spies' sin retold and the oath of the forty years; they offer to arm and cross first, and Moses stipulates it doubled, if you cross, this land is yours; if not, you have sinned, charges Eleazar and Joshua with it, and gives the land now; they build their cities, and Machir, Jair and Nobah take theirs. The machine writes the condition as two debits on the compound party, one closed when the cities are built and one open to Joshua 22, writes the grant as three transfers valued at the two and a half tribes' count read off the population table, and records the negative arm with the Talmud's two positions. ## 43. Masei (journeys). Numbers 33:1-36:13

### Records the itinerary of forty-two stations written at the run's end, with Aaron's death dated to the day and the judgments on Egypt's gods told here alone; then the command to dispossess and to destroy the figured stones, its negative arm a warning.

The machine writes the list as one status on the people with the forty-two as its value, checks the departure's date against the exodus marker and Aaron's against the tape's own marker built from this verse, writes the judgments on the gods as the run of Exodus 12:12 told nowhere else, and leaves the command's two debits open by design to Joshua's runs. The tradition's chronology counts a retreat of seven stations after Aaron's death, and Moserah stands seven camps before Mount Hor on the list; the Talmud proves the era's New Year from this date with Deuteronomy 1:3. The figured stone's own ban in Leviticus 26:1 is a call filed as owed.

### Installs the land's four borders as one status on the land, the lot cited and not rewritten, and the twelve dividers named as rows in the population table.

The machine writes the four sides as one status on the land of Canaan, cites the lot's debit of chapter 26 a third time without rewriting it, reads the grant back rather than writing it again, and names the twelve dividers as rows in the population table, the first register paid by rows since the table was built. Judah's south border in Joshua is this chapter's south side, twenty of thirty-three tokens.

### Installs the Levites' forty-eight cities with their two thousand cubits and the six cities of refuge: the murderer's table by instrument, manner and intent; the manslayer's term until the death of the high priest, an entry closed by a death, not a timer; the two witnesses; no ransom; and the land that blood pollutes. The machine writes the two gifts as debits on the people open by design to Joshua 20 and 21, builds the case table from the chapter's tokens, and writes the manslayer's term as an open entry carrying the name of the priest in office, closed when that priest's death is read, Eleazar's is Joshua 24:33, with no timer anywhere; the parser was taught the bare "two thousand" here, its last rule. The Sifrei returns at 35:9 for its last three sections, the Mishnah's Makkot is the answer sheet whole, and the Talmud reaches the Sabbath's two thousand cubits from this chapter's measure by a chain of eight analogies. "In whose midst I dwell" stands at 5:3 and here alone in the Torah, the book's inclusio computed.

### Records the tribe's second plea about the daughters, answered without a halt: they marry within their father's tribe, and the book's commands close in the plains of Moab.

The heads of Gilead's fathers come to Moses: if Zelophehad's daughters marry out of the tribe, their inheritance goes with them and the jubilee will not bring it back; Moses answers by the mouth of the LORD that the tribe of Joseph speaks rightly, and the daughters marry their cousins, their inheritance staying with their father's tribe; these are the commandments the LORD commanded in the plains of Moab. The machine does not halt here, because the rule from the first plea is already in force and this plea only bounds it: the answer, relayed in Moses' mouth, installs a cell inside the daughters' law, the transfer between tribes barred for that generation, and the marriage is the run "as the LORD commanded Moses." The Talmud reads "this is the thing" as binding that generation alone, with the fifteenth of Av as the day the tribes were let marry into one another. The book's footer carries its place-stamp, and the Sifrei's Hebrew colophon, the book of Numbers is completed, is dropped by the English.
