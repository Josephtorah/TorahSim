# Attribution and data provenance

TorahSim builds on the work of others. This file records exactly what came
from where, and under what terms.

## Hebrew text — Westminster Leningrad Codex (public domain)

The Hebrew text of the Tanakh (the 24 books of the Hebrew Bible) in
`data/tanakh.sqlite` is the **Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC)**, the
electronic transcription of the Leningrad Codex (the oldest complete
manuscript of the Hebrew Bible, ca. 1008 CE) maintained by the
J. Alan Groves Center for Advanced Biblical Research. The WLC text is in
the **public domain**.

## Lemma and morphology tags — Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible (CC BY 4.0)

The word-level Strong's lemma numbers and morphology codes attached to the
Hebrew text come from the **Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible (OSHB)** project:

  https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb

OSHB data is licensed **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International
(CC BY 4.0)**. It is redistributed here under that license, with thanks.
If you redistribute the data in `data/tanakh.sqlite`, carry this notice
forward.

## English glosses — Strong's Concordance (public domain) + project glosses

The word-by-word English glosses (the interlinear layer) are derived from
**Strong's Exhaustive Concordance of the Bible** (James Strong, 1890,
public domain), corrected and supplemented by hand glosses written for this
project (CC BY 4.0, this repository). The glosses are a reading aid, not a
translation.

## Chapter translation in the scroll reader — JPS 1917 (public domain)

The whole-verse English lines in the scroll reader (`scroll/`) are from
**The Holy Scriptures According to the Masoretic Text** (Jewish
Publication Society, 1917), in the **public domain**. Where the JPS
versification differs from the text's (three Decalogue-region chapters),
the reader falls back to the word-gloss chain rather than misalign a
translation.

## Oral-law sources — cited, not redistributed

The scan records and claim manifests cite and briefly quote works of the
oral-law tradition — the Mishnah, the Tosefta, the halakhic midrashim
(Mekhilta, Sifra, Sifrei), the two Talmuds, and later commentators — by
standard reference (tractate, chapter, section). The original
Hebrew/Aramaic texts of these works are ancient and in the public domain;
this repository does not redistribute any modern edition or translation
of them.
