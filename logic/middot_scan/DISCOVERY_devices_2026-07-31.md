## Classification (human pass, 2026-07-31)

**A. NEW DEVICE DISCOVERIES — operators on no list we scanned, surfaced by mining:**

1. ***Ein ketiv kan ela*** ("it is NOT written here … BUT rather") — **39 sources**,
   the run's star find: the chain's minimal-pair **diff operator** — contrasting what
   the verse could have said against what it says, and deriving from the difference.
   This is the classical form of our own spec-delta method: the machine diffs order
   against delivery; the chain diffs expected wording against actual wording. Same
   move, two instruments, independently converged.
2. ***Kemah de-at amar*** ("as you say [elsewhere]," Aramaic) — **22 sources**: the
   usage-analogy operator — a word's sense imported from its usage in another verse.
   A lighter cousin of the verbal analogy, common in Bereshit Rabbah ("Great
   Genesis") and the Jerusalem Talmud.
3. ***Ve-ein X ela Y*** ("X means nothing but Y") — visible in the mined rows on our
   own test token ("*ve-ein tov ela…*" — "'good' means nothing but…"): the **lexical
   definition operator**, fixing a word's sense corpus-wide by decree + prooftext.
4. ***Lekhakh ne'emar*** ("therefore it is stated") — **32 sources**: the derivation
   **closer** — marks the arrival back at the verse after an argument.
5. ***Ve-khen hu omer*** ("and so it says") — **25 sources**: the **corroboration
   operator** — a second prooftext stacked on the first.
6. ***Be-shem* attribution chains** ("Rabbi X in the name of Rabbi Y") — the
   **transmission-provenance operator**: the chain's own metadata layer, naming the
   tradent path of each teaching. (Our provenance register is this, formalized.)
7. **The two time-registers** — *ba-olam ha-zeh* ("in this world," 35 sources) /
   *le-atid lavo* ("in the future to come," 28): the narrative side's paired
   **temporal re-anchoring** operators.
8. ***Ve-lo od ela*** ("and not only that, but…," 16) and *af al pi* ("even
   though," 18): argument **connectives** (escalation; concession).

**B. Confirmations of the known lexicon (detector cross-check):** *davar acher*
("another interpretation") tops the table at 60 sources; *zeh she-amar ha-katuv*
("this is what Scripture said") 27; *al achat kamah ve-khamah* ("how much more so")
22 — the mining independently re-finds the v1.1 formula list, which validates the
method.

**C. Named-device scan:** 10 of 17 curated known-but-unlisted devices present, led
by *yakhol … talmud lomar* ("one might think … Scripture teaches") in **76
sources** — the hypothesis-rejection structure is the single most widespread device
in our cache, ahead of everything on either canonical list. The 7 absentees
(*semukhin* "adjacency," *asmakhta* "support text," *im eino inyan* "if not needed
here," etc.) are legal-sugya devices — their absence measures our cache's
Genesis-narrative bias, not their nonexistence. They should appear when Leviticus
triage begins; their arrival will be a coverage indicator.

**D. Noise classes (excluded):** printed chapter-verse citations ("bereshit 1 14,"
bare numeral pairs), dialogue furniture (*amar leih* "he said to him"), divine
epithets, and near-verse fragments that survived the quotation filter via spelling
variants.

**Bottom line:** the two canonical lists (13 + 32) cover the *inference* core, but
the working language is larger — a citation apparatus, a diff operator, a lexical
definer, provenance chains, time-registers, and argument connectives. All of it is
formulaic, therefore all of it is parseable. The solid discoveries move into the
detector lexicon tagged `discovered` (v1.2).
