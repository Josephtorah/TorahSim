## Onkelos Exodus 27:1-21 — read IN FULL, 21 verses

- Onkelos Exod 27:3 — MATERIAL (light). לדשנו ("for its ashes")
  rendered לְמִסְפֵי קִטְמֵיהּ ("to REMOVE its ashes") — the
  ash-clearance duty (the dawn service's first act) named in the
  vessel list; the pots, shovels, basins, forks, and fire-pans all
  bronze. TESTING-shelf pointer: Mishnah Eruvin 10:15's row anchors
  on this verse in the enumeration.
- Onkelos Exod 27:8 — context-plus. נבוב לחת ("hollow, of boards")
  rendered חֲלִיל לוּחֵי ("HOLLOW of boards") — the altar a shell,
  not a solid; and the shown-pattern clause a third time: כְּמָא דִי
  אַחֲזִי יָתָךְ בְּטוּרָא כֵּן יַעְבְּדוּן ("as He showed you in the
  mountain, SO SHALL THEY MAKE" — the doers now plural).
- Onkelos Exod 27:9 — MATERIAL (light). The courtyard's hangings —
  קלעים rendered סְרָדִין — the CURTAIN-BOUNDARY the case shelf uses
  as a legal line: most-holy offerings eaten "outside the curtains"
  is a lash row (Mishnah Makkot 3:3, READ IN FULL this morning in the
  backfill exam ledger — its boundary-word is this verse's own
  fixture; credit, not re-read).
- Onkelos Exod 27:18 — MATERIAL, the chapter's crown. The court's
  measure: מְאָה בְאַמִּין ("a hundred cubits") long, חַמְשִׁין
  בְּחַמְשִׁין ("fifty by fifty") wide, five cubits high — the
  100×50 enclosure the tradition takes as its STANDARD AREA UNIT:
  בית סאתים ("the space of two seah") — the Sabbath-carrying
  enclosure limit — is defined as "like the courtyard of the
  tabernacle" (the TESTING-shelf row Mishnah Eruvin 2:5 anchors here:
  every karpef and enclosure in Sabbath law is measured against THIS
  verse's court). Architecture becoming a unit of measure.
- Onkelos Exod 27:20 — MATERIAL (Tetzaveh's opening, in-span). מִשְׁחָא
  זֵיתָא דַכְיָא כָּתִישָׁא ("PURE OLIVE oil, CRUSHED") לְאַנְהָרָא
  ("for the light") — the crushed-grade requirement the case shelf
  grades: כתית למאור ("crushed for the LIGHT — not crushed for the
  meal-offerings"): the stricter grade for the lamp only
  (TESTING-shelf pointer: Mishnah Menachot 8:5, the oil-grades table,
  anchors here). תְּדִירָא ("CONTINUALLY") — the tamid-token again.
- Onkelos Exod 27:21 — MATERIAL. The lamp-tending duty installed:
  Aaron and his sons order it מֵרַמְשָׁא עַד צַפְרָא ("from evening
  to morning") before the LORD — חקת עולם ("an everlasting statute")
  rendered קְיָם עָלָם ("an EVERLASTING STATUTE/COVENANT-BOND") —
  לְדָרֵיהוֹן ("for their generations"): the parashah's closing
  clause is a perpetual duty, the priesthood's first standing
  assignment, given before the priesthood itself is clothed (that is
  the next portion).
- Onkelos Exod 27:1-2, 27:4-7, 27:10-17, 27:19 — context (the altar
  five-by-five square and three high, its horns FROM it, the bronze
  overlay, the net-grate to its half-height, the poles, the court's
  pillars and silver bands, the twenty-cubit gate screen of
  embroiderer's work, the bronze pegs).

## SPINE AMENDMENT, same day (the delegated ruling — see the exo_25
## ledger's header note): Midrash Tanchuma joins Onkelos for Exod
## 25-40. The altar sections attributed here, read IN FULL.

- Midrash Tanchuma, Terumah 10 — MATERIAL (light), the letter and
  measure correspondences. מזבח ("altar") read as an acrostic —
  מחילה זכות ברכה חיים ("pardon, merit, blessing, life"); שטים
  likewise (peace, good, salvation, pardon); the five-by-five against
  the five-and-five of the two tablets; the three cubits of height
  against the three deliverers (Micah 6:4's Moses, Aaron, Miriam);
  the four horns against the four horns raised at Sinai. The
  letter-arithmetic family (the Kitzur's class), recorded as such.
- Midrash Tanchuma, Terumah 11 — MATERIAL, a recorded engineering
  objection AT THE SPEC. Moses to the Holy One: You said an
  acacia-wood altar plated bronze, and You said אש תמיד תוקד ("a
  PERPETUAL FIRE shall burn on the altar," Leviticus 6:6) — the fire
  will pass the plating and burn the wood! The answer stack: the
  measures above are not yours (fire-angels under water-firmaments,
  עושה שלום במרומיו — "He makes peace in His heights"); the
  precedents — dead things enter before Me and leave alive (Aaron's
  staff that flowered, Numbers 17:23; Solomon's cedars that bore
  fruit until Manasseh), and Moses himself walked into the fire (our
  own 20:21 quoted — "and Moses drew near the thick cloud"). R.
  Nechemiah's datum: the plating was עובי דינר ("a DINAR'S
  THICKNESS"). And the reason-layer: bronze to atone for מצחך נחושה
  ("your BRAZEN forehead," Isaiah 48:4). Moses' altar dearer than
  Solomon's — Solomon kept the bronze altar's NAME on his own
  twenty-cubit altar (2 Chronicles 4:1).
