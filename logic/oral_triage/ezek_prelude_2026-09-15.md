## 1. THE TWO PRINCIPLES (the owner's words)

- "Law is code. Let's keep that in mind." A legal passage in ANY book is a procedure to compile, never a description to summarize; the
  prophets' rules included. A later book's rule is a spec with its own constants, to be run and diverge-checked against the Torah's.
- "Law is code and narrative is data, variables, and whatever else it does." Narrative is the tape's event stream; its numbers the
  variables the code reads; its date stamps the markers; its acts the calls that fire the code and the switches that install it; its
  receipt formulas the closes. The one reverse flow is the tent: a case with no law produces a rule in its own name.

## 2. THE SCAN (measured)

- 48 chapters, 1,273 verses, 18,730 words. Two books in one: chapters 1-39 a RUN, chapters 40-48 a SPEC.
- The run's formulas: "son of man" 94; "the word of the LORD came to me" 49; "thus says the Lord GOD" 122; "you shall know that I am the
  LORD" 58 (the prophets' receipt formula, the run's closing line); fourteen date stamps (1:1-2, 8:1, 20:1, 24:1, 26:1, 29:1, 29:17,
  30:20, 31:1, 32:1, 32:17, 33:21, 40:1). 40:1 carries TWO ERAS in one stamp: the twenty-fifth year of the captivity and the fourteenth
  after the city was smitten.
- The spec's density: "cubit" 46, "measured" 35, "the reed" 12, all in 40-47; number words 343 in chapters 40-48 against 120 in the other
  thirty-nine chapters combined (chapter 48 alone 121). "Son of man" nearly stops after chapter 44.
- "A day for a year" as one four-word phrase stands at exactly two seats in the Bible: Numbers 14:34 (forty days become forty years) and
  Ezekiel 4:6 (390 and 40 days stand for years). Numbers compresses; Ezekiel decompresses, in the same words.
- The vocabulary of the spec is only partly the Torah's: chapters 40-43 share 40 of their 136 rare words (100 seats or fewer) with the
  tabernacle spec of Exodus 25-31 and 35-40, and 42 of them appear nowhere in the Torah; chapters 44-46 share 46 with the offering law
  (Leviticus 1-7, 23; Numbers 28-29) and 31 with the priests' law (Leviticus 21-22; Numbers 18); chapters 47-48 share the borders with
  Numbers 34 (Hamath, "the side", "the border", "southward") and the tribe list with Joshua 13-19. Ezekiel does not decompress the
  Torah whole; it re-instantiates records inside it with different constants.

## 3. THE LOGIC FOUND (recursion, reference, loops, nesting — measured)

- DEFINE ONCE, POINT BACK: chapter 10 recalls chapter 1 with 21 of its 33 rare words; "wheel within the wheel" at exactly 1:16 and 10:10;
  the later visions are pointers, "like the vision which I saw," at 8:4, 10:22, 11:24 and 43:3 (the last pointing at two earlier visions;
  the river Chebar named eight times as the anchor). The gates of chapter 40: the east gate measured room by room, then "the first gate"
  (40:21) and "according to these measures" six times (40:24, 28, 29, 32, 33, 35) for the north, the south and the three inner gates.
  One copy stored, the rest references; the reader decompresses by copying.
- A FUNCTION OVER GENERATIONS: chapter 18 defines the righteous man by a list of conditions (18:5-9), applies it to the violent son
  (18:10-13, 7 of the 12 rare words reused) and to the son's righteous son (18:14-17, 9 reused), states the base case ("the soul that
  sins, it shall die"; nothing passes between generations), then the transitions (18:21-28). The same function is called at 3:17-21 and
  in chapter 33 for the watchman: "he shall surely live" at nine seats across the three, "he shall surely die" at four.
- A LOOP OVER THE NATION: chapter 20 runs the cycle "brought out, rebelled, statutes and sabbaths given, fury withheld for My name's sake"
  three times (the fathers in Egypt, the wilderness generation, their children), sharing 27 of its 54 rare words with Exodus 12-20 and
  Numbers 13-20, "my sabbaths" in six verses, "in the wilderness" in nine; and it ends with an operation, 20:37-38, "pass under the rod
  ... purge out the rebels ... they shall not enter the land": a FILTER over the population.
- A TEMPLATE IN A LOOP: chapter 48 writes "one" (portion) thirteen times, once per tribe; the river of chapter 47 is "measured a thousand"
  at 47:3, 47:4 (twice) and 47:5, four steps with one rising state variable until the stop condition; the city's gates three per side.
- NESTING: the sanctuary's five-hundred square (42:17-20; 45:2) inside the priests' ten-thousand strip (45:1, 3, 5; 48:9, 10, 13, 18)
  inside the holy portion inside the tribal strips; the city's own five hundreds at 48:16, 30-34.

## 4. THE FILING (as the machine would file it)

CODE, what compiles: 18 and 33:10-20 (the decision procedure; called at 3:17-21 and 33:1-9 for the watchman); 44:9-31 (the priests'
rules) and 45:9-12 (the CONSTANTS: an honest ephah and bath "of one measure", the shekel twenty gerahs, the maneh; declared before the
schedule that uses them, as Exodus 30 set the shekel before the census); 45:18-46:15 (the schedule; the east gate shut six days and open
on the sabbath and the new moon, a calendar-keyed state; 46:9 the routing rule, no one leaves by the gate he entered); 46:16-18 (the
prince's inheritance law, calling Leviticus 25 by name: "until the year of liberty"); 40-43 and 47:13-48 (the construction and the
partition, opened by a UNIT DEFINITION at 40:5, a reed of six cubits of a cubit and a handbreadth; the altar's seven-day consecration at
43:18-27 as Leviticus 8's form).

DATA, everything else, and what each piece does: MARKERS (the fourteen dates; 24:2 the text ORDERING a marker written, "write the name of
the day, this selfsame day", the siege's start; 33:21 the fugitive's dated "the city is smitten", the marker that closes what 24:2 opened;
29:17 the book's latest date, out of order); EVENTS AS MODELS, the sign-acts as scenarios run before history runs them (4:1-3 a brick
with siege works and an iron plate; 4:4-8 the timer; 4:9-11 the rations as parameters, twenty shekels a day, a sixth of a hin; 5:1-4 the
hair weighed on balances and cut in thirds, a partition; 12 the exile's baggage; 24 the pot; 37:16-19 two sticks merged into one);
DEBITS AND RECEIPTS (each oracle against a nation opens a debt, chapters 25-32; "you shall know that I am the LORD" the receipt, 58);
A LEDGER OPERATION IN THE INK, 29:18-20: Nebuchadnezzar's army "had no wages" from Tyre, so Egypt is given him "as wages for his army",
a debt unpaid by one party reassigned to another; CALLS (the visions as pointers to chapter 1; 22:6-12 the Torah's prohibitions read off
as a checklist against the city: blood, bribes, usury, oppression).

THE TENT, where data makes code, three seats: 14:1-8 (the elders come to inquire, a case brought; the answer a standing rule for "every
man of the house of Israel"); 18:2 (the people's proverb the case, the whole decision procedure its output); 44:1-3 (the east gate "shall
be shut ... because the LORD entered by it": the glory's passage at 43:4, an event, becomes a permanent rule about a door).

## 5. THE RIVER (47:1-12) — the owner's reading, and the tradition's

The passage as verbs: out from under the threshold beside the altar; measure a thousand, wade, ankle-deep; measure, wade, knee-deep;
measure, wade, to the loins; measure, "a river that I could not pass over ... waters to swim in"; then the verb changes: "he brought me
back to the BANK of the river," and only from the bank is the rest seen: the trees on both sides, the sea healed, the fish "as the fish of
the great sea," fruit every month, and the marshes not healed, "given to salt." The owner's reading: the temple is the code, measured once,
small; the river is the data leaving it, growing by equal measures until the reader can no longer wade it; the answer in the text is the
bank, the view over the water, not the water (the board and the database over the ledger). "Everything shall live whither the river comes"
is the ledger reaching a thing; the fish by kinds the population table filling; the monthly fruit a period timer; the healed sea the
receipts; the salt marshes the entries the run never closes, kept open by design. THE SHELF READ THE DEPTHS THE SAME WAY: Yoma 77b:12-15
uses 47:3-5 as the scale of what may be crossed on the fast day, up to the ankles yes, "waters to swim in" not by swimming, not by a
small boat, not by a great ship (Isaiah 33:21); Sanhedrin 100a:6 names the river, from the Holy of Holies, every kind of fruit, new each
month. The caveat, once: the tradition's referent is the sanctuary's blessing, not data; the structure is the text's, the mapping ours.

## 7. THE SCANS (reproducible; run from the repo root; the database is Data/tanakh.sqlite; book code 'Ezek')

```python
import sqlite3, re, collections
db = sqlite3.connect('Data/tanakh.sqlite')
L = lambda lm: re.sub(r'[^0-9]', '', (lm or '').split('/')[-1])
rows = db.execute("select v.book, v.chapter, v.verse, w.lemma from verses v join words w on w.verse_id=v.id order by v.id, w.idx").fetchall()
seq = collections.defaultdict(list)
for b, c, v, lm in rows: seq[(b, c, v)].append(L(lm))
def where(ph, book=None):                      # the seats of an adjacent lemma phrase across the Tanakh (or one book)
    n = len(ph); return sorted('%s %d:%d' % k for k, s in seq.items() if (book is None or k[0] == book) and any(s[i:i+n] == ph for i in range(len(s) - n + 1)))
print(where(['3117', '8141', '3117', '8141']))          # "a day for a year": Num 14:34, Ezek 4:6
print(where(['212', '8432', '212']))                    # "wheel within the wheel": Ezek 1:16, 10:10
print(where(['4060', '428'], 'Ezek'))                    # "according to these measures": the six gates by reference
print(where(['4058', '505'], 'Ezek'))                    # "measured a thousand": the river's steps 47:3-5
print(where(['2421', '2421'], 'Ezek'), where(['4191', '4191'], 'Ezek'))   # "he shall surely live" / "he shall surely die": 3, 18, 33
```
