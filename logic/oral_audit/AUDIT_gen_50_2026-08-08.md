## CROWNS (chain-attested + DB-verified)

- **G50-01 the wage-verb against two anomalies**: אעבדך ("I will
  SERVE YOU seven years for Rachel," 29:18) — bet in plain qamatz,
  NOT the Chizkuni's compound pointing; the root's first letter
  standard, NOT the Jerusalem manuscript's reading Minchat Shai
  calls "a great anomaly"; one pashta, on the final letter, none
  on the bet. The promise-verb twin banked at Bethel (28:15,
  "I will not leave you") lands: SNAPSHOT sides with Minchat Shai
  at both stations of his own cross-reference.
- **G50-03 the tender eyes stand as written**: רכות ("and Leah's
  eyes were TENDER," 29:17) — the Torah's ONLY token of the word,
  written with no aleph: Ben Efraim's emendation ("read it LONG")
  refuted by the letter itself — Minchat Shai: "he lacked nothing
  but wisdom" — while the enduring-gifts derash (priesthood,
  Levites, kingship) rides the word as written; the Kitzur's
  soft-words pair is Writings-side, named.
- **G50-04 the two verse-initial eyes**: ועיני ("and the eyes
  of...") opens a verse exactly TWICE in the Torah — Leah's
  tender eyes (29:17) and ISRAEL's age-heavy eyes (48:10): the
  woman who feared marrying Esau and the man her eyes feared for,
  grown old, blessing her rival's grandsons — the Kitzur's third
  (the failing eyes of the wicked) named, Writings-side.
- **G50-05/06 the demand and its purpose, both marked**: הבה
  ("GIVE my wife") end-stressed as ruled; ואבואה ("that I may
  COME to her") — the form's ONE full-spelled Torah token, per
  the rule "full with vav, all the rest lean": the demanded
  wedding carries the Torah's one full coming.
- **G50-02/07/08/10 pointing as ruled**: the-younger's dageshed
  nun (29:18); the accusation's hard zayin ("what is THIS you
  have done," 29:25 — the deceived-accuser formula in Jacob's
  mouth); the also-words of the double marriage dageshed and
  joined (29:27, 29:30); the fulfillment-verb's tzere lamed
  (29:28).
- **G50-11 the second to-him stands**: 29:28 carries לו ("to
  him") twice — some books drop the second; the codices of Spain
  and the books of R. Meir and R. Gershom keep it, and SNAPSHOT
  keeps both. The doubled to-him lands on the verse that finally
  pops the give-my-wife demand: the letter says twice whose wife
  she is.
